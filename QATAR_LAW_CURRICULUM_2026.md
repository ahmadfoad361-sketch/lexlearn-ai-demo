# LexLearn — Qatar Year-1 Sources of Obligations Curriculum Map (2026)

## Official programs checked

### Qatar University — College of Law
- Bachelor of Laws, first year, Spring: **LAWC 213 — Sources of Obligations**, 3 credit hours, prerequisite LAWC 101.
- Official course scope covers: concept/types of obligations; contract; consent; subject matter; cause; defects of consent; invalidity; contractual liability; pacta sunt servanda; good faith; unforeseen circumstances; termination; unilateral will; tort; unjust enrichment; legislation.
- Official sources:
  - https://www.qu.edu.qa/ar/Colleges/law/programs/programs/Pages/corse.aspx
  - https://www.qu.edu.qa/en-us/Colleges/law/departments/private-law/Pages/course-description.aspx
  - https://qttsc.qu.edu.qa/en-us/students/Documents/undergraduate-student-catalog-2025-2026-en.pdf

### Lusail University — College of Law
- Bachelor of Law, first year, Spring: **LAWC 104 — Sources of Obligations**, 3 credit hours.
- The official study plan places it in the same year/semester band as Qatar University's course.
- Lusail University Press publishes the teaching book **The General Theory of Obligations in Qatari Civil Law — Book One: Sources of Obligations** (2024), covering Qatari Civil Code arts. 64–240 and organizing the sources into five principal parts.
- Official sources:
  - https://lu.edu.qa/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Law-Study-Plan.pdf
  - https://lu.edu.qa/book11.php

### HBKU College of Law
- HBKU does not offer an undergraduate LLB; its JD is graduate-entry.
- First-year JD includes **LAW 651 Contract Law** and **LAW 652 Injury Law/Torts**, useful as advanced comparative enrichment, not as the year-1 LLB baseline.
- Official source:
  - https://catalog.hbku.edu.qa/academic-degrees/law/jd/

## Shared Qatar year-1 core for LexLearn

The production course should cover these blocks:
1. Concept, characteristics and types of obligations.
2. Contract as a voluntary source.
3. Contract formation: offer/acceptance, consent, subject matter, cause, legal formalities.
4. Capacity and defects of consent.
5. Invalidity and its consequences.
6. Effects of contract: binding force and good-faith performance.
7. Contractual liability and termination.
8. Unilateral will, including promise of reward.
9. Tort liability: personal act, acts of others, damage caused by things.
10. Unjust enrichment and applications such as payment not due / management of another's affairs where appropriate to the syllabus.
11. Legislation as a direct source.
12. Integrated issue spotting, source classification, legal precision and exam-answer construction.
