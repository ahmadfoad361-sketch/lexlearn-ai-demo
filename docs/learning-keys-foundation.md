# LexLearn — Learning Keys and Double-Weakness Foundation
Date: 2026-10-16. Status: educational prototype implementation, not an efficacy claim.

## Goal
Support the two evidence-informed paths **understanding → retrieval** and **recalled knowledge → explanation/application** without reducing learners to fixed learning styles. A student's scores are provisional task-performance indicators that update after new responses.

## Foundation pathway: both recall and understanding are weak
- Trigger only if there is actual measured evidence for both dimensions and both scores are below 45 (threshold is an engineering starting point requiring piloting).
- Do not assume the learner has an established strong skill. Show an accessible source-grounded worked example, a supported decision, then ask for unsupervised recall, explanation, and transfer to another case.
- Keep skill evidence distinct: viewing examples or reading notes does not earn a score. Written answers pending legal/semantic review remain unscored.
- Re-evaluate the double-weakness condition as new evidence arrives. Outside the foundation, keep the original strength-to-weakness bridge.

## Learning Keys notebook
- A student-owned persistent learning aid in the student programme, distinct from a test and separate from the source textbook/course.
- Keys are short cues tied to a *meaning/explanation* and a *legal source reference*. Provide trusted example cues only for topics actually released for the Qatar pilot (sessions 1 and 22); other cues are student-authored and clearly unverified.
- Store data inside the existing per-student course snapshot. No new unprotected table or cross-student data flow. Guest-only data stays local on the device.
- Notebook editable, visible between tasks and after a session; not displayed or accessible from within assessment screens, including weekly gates.
- Mark self-written or pending-review cues as personal/unverified; **not** proof of mastery. A change to an approved cue must not inherit approval status.
- Allow prompts for studying from notes (view cue, hide explanation, try reconstructing). No notebook interaction writes grade evidence or advances mastery.
- Student must return to source text, retrieval without hints, explanation, new facts, and structured exam response for actual achievement.

## Course and instructor
The university's designated primary course materials and instructor remain authoritative. LexLearn adapts supplementary study practice and does not replace teaching or give independent certification of legal correctness.

## Academic review / remaining validation
A law instructor must audit the reference material, legal wording, proposed cues, and fact variants. Validate automated grading with real diverse answers, including Arabic variants. Use a preregistered study with delayed recall and unseen transfer tasks. There is no justified global 'scientific compliance percentage' or proven LexLearn-specific effect yet.

## Research support (principles; not product validation)
- Craik & Tulving 1975, semantic encoding: https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.104.3.268
- Chi et al. 1994, self-explanation: https://doi.org/10.1016/0364-0213(94)90016-7
- Roediger & Karpicke 2006, retrieval practice: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x
- Karpicke & Blunt 2011, comprehension/inference: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199327
- Butler 2010, transfer: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019902
- Cepeda et al. 2006, spacing: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354
- Dunlosky et al. 2013, comparative technique review: https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266

## Do not overclaim
Dunlosky et al. rated isolated keyword mnemonic as low-utility in general, whereas self-explanation moderate and practice testing/spacing high. Therefore the notebook is not a mnemonic cure: cue + explanation + unguided retrieval + feedback + later review is the complete learning loop.
