## One-line description
LexLearn is an adaptive legal-learning platform that diagnoses how law students recall, understand, apply and retain legal material, then turns that diagnosis into a personalised study path and exam-answer training.

## 40-word pitch
LexLearn helps law students study smarter by measuring recall, understanding, application and retention, then adapting what they learn next. It combines sourced legal content, personalised practice and structured exam-answer training, starting with Qatar and Egypt as separate legal systems.

## Short pitch (approx. 80 words)
Law students often receive the same notes, questions and revision path even when their learning gaps are completely different. LexLearn diagnoses whether a student’s difficulty is recall, understanding, application, retention or exam execution, then adapts the next activity accordingly. The working prototype separates Qatar and Egypt legal content, starts with core civil-law subjects, and turns diagnostic evidence into a personalised study strategy and structured exam-answer practice.

## Problem
Most legal-learning tools treat performance as a single score. A student who remembers a rule but cannot apply it is treated similarly to a student who understands the concept but cannot retain the wording. This wastes study time and makes revision less targeted, especially in law where recall, interpretation, application and exam structure all matter.

## Solution
LexLearn uses short legal-text tasks, immediate and delayed recall, comprehension questions, fact-variation exercises and exam-answer scaffolding. It builds a skill profile, identifies the likely learning gap, and recommends the next activity. The aim is not to label students, but to use evidence to choose a more efficient study route.

## What makes LexLearn different?
1. Legal learning is decomposed into distinct skills rather than one score.
2. The platform separates legal content by jurisdiction, so Qatar and Egypt do not share legal sources or questions.
3. Immediate performance is distinguished from delayed retention.
4. Students are trained to convert legal knowledge into structured exam answers.
5. Source identity is preserved: legislation, official material, historical exams and study notes are not treated as equivalent evidence.

## Market opportunity
LexLearn begins with legal education because law has a particularly clear need to distinguish recall, conceptual understanding, application and exam execution. The adaptive engine can later extend to other high-content professional disciplines where students must remember, interpret and apply structured rules.

## Qatar opportunity
Qatar is an ideal first pilot market because the product can be localised to the actual legal curriculum and legislation while remaining technically scalable. The initial Qatar track uses a separate legal-content layer and can be reviewed with local academics before any formal pilot.

## Competition / alternatives
Alternatives include:
- LMS quiz systems
- Generic AI tutors
- Law question banks
- Static exam-preparation platforms
- General adaptive-learning systems

LexLearn’s differentiation is the combination of legal-domain skill diagnosis, delayed retention, jurisdiction-specific sourcing, adaptive intervention, and exam-answer construction.

## Use of AI
AI is used as a supporting layer for adaptation, classification and learning-path assistance. It is not intended to replace academic judgement, invent legal sources, or make high-stakes decisions about students. The product is designed around traceable source identity and human review.
